NO KINGS
🖨️ Print / PDF

The realm of Cardlandia is fracturing. Topple the old regime, claim the glory — but if tyranny secures a stronghold before the revolution succeeds, the crown wins. First to 50 points founds the new order.

The Cast & the Deck

A standard 52-card deck with the court renamed, plus 4 wild Jokers.

CardWho they are
Kings (K)Absolute Monarchs. Tyrants never hide — the instant you draw a King you must play it face-up in front of you.
Queens (Q)Cunning Plotters, scheming from the shadow of your hand.
Generals (10)The Military Brass — loyal to command, coup, or chaos.
Bishops (A)The Holy Order, wielding sanction against the Crown.
The Crowd (2–9)The Common Folk. Weak alone, devastating united.
Jacks (J)Ambitious Heirs waiting in the wings. Jacks can never be discarded.

Players — default 4 (Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades). For 5–6 players the working guilds join with a full suit each: the 5th is Anvils (green), the 6th is Wheat (blue).

The Revolutionary Cycle

  1. The oldest player takes the first turn; play proceeds clockwise.
  2. Draft support — draw one card. Draw a King and you crown yourself at once, face-up on the table.
  3. Maintain order — hand limit is 7. Discard the excess out of play, but ambitious Jacks refuse to be discarded.
  4. Strike or plot — launch a strike to topple standing Kings, or hold your cards for a larger coup.

Three Revolutionary Paths

Every takedown is one of three easy ideas. A toppled King becomes a Fallen King in your victory pile; the cards used are discarded.

Revolt is a run of consecutive same-suit number cards 2–9 — no faces inside the run — all aimed at one player's Kings. A Bishop may lead the march for flavor, but adds no count.

The Second Wave (Jack's ambition)

  • When every true King has fallen, the ambitious Jacks instantly rise as Promoted Kings — everyone reveals their Jacks at once.
  • A single General can crush a Promoted King (discarded).
  • Joker + General is a swift assassination: remove a Promoted King and claim the Jack as a Promoted Fallen King.
  • Bishops, pairs, and Revolts topple Promoted Kings by their normal rules.

The Grand Reckoning (scoring)

Generals' coup: hold all 4 Generals when the 4th King is played and you may claim the +10 instead of whoever raised the Kings.

Ending a Round

A round ends when either a player has 4 Kings standing (a tyrannical dynasty is secured — a loss for society, a win for the individual), or the draw deck is played through once. Tally scores, reshuffle, next round. First to 50 wins. The "four" thresholds stay at four even in 5–6 player games.
